"Net worth" sounds like a number for people with yachts, which is exactly why most people never calculate their own — but it's just a subtraction problem, and it's the single most useful number for actually knowing whether your finances are headed in the right direction.

## The one-sentence version

Net worth is everything you own (assets) minus everything you owe (debts/liabilities) — a single number that captures your entire financial position at once, regardless of whether it's positive, negative, small, or large.

<!--more-->

## How to actually calculate it

**Add up your assets** — cash in checking and savings, your [emergency fund]({{ '/emergency-fund-how-much/' | relative_url }}), investment and retirement account balances, and (if you want to include it) the estimated value of things like a car or a house.

**Add up your liabilities** — credit card balances, student loans, a car loan, a mortgage, any other debt you owe.

**Subtract.** That's it. A 24-year-old with $8,000 in savings and investments and $15,000 in student loans has a net worth of **−$7,000**. That's not a crisis or a failure — it's an honest, specific number, and specific numbers are useful precisely because you can watch them change.

## Why a negative number isn't the point

A negative net worth is extremely common early in adulthood — student loans, a car loan, or early-career low savings can all put the number below zero, and that's a normal starting point, not a red flag by itself. The number itself, especially early on, matters far less than **which direction it's moving**. Someone with −$10,000 net worth that improves by $5,000 a year is in a fundamentally healthier position than someone with +$20,000 net worth that's been declining for three years, even though the second person's raw number currently looks better.

## Why this is more useful than tracking any single account

Checking your investment balance alone, or your credit card balance alone, gives you a fragment — it's easy to feel good about a growing investment account while ignoring a credit card balance quietly climbing right alongside it. Net worth forces everything onto one line: growth in your [index fund]({{ '/index-funds-vs-individual-stocks/' | relative_url }}) and a shrinking emergency fund and a creeping credit card balance all net out into one honest number, instead of three separate stories you can choose to look at selectively.

## What actually moves the number, and by how much

Three things change your net worth, and they're not equally controllable:

- **Paying down debt** moves the number dollar-for-dollar, guaranteed, the moment the payment posts — no market risk involved.
- **Saving and investing new money** moves it dollar-for-dollar going in, then continues moving with [compounding]({{ '/what-is-compound-interest/' | relative_url }}) and market performance from there.
- **Market movement on existing investments** moves the number up or down on its own, which is also why a single bad month shouldn't be read as a verdict on your overall financial trajectory — it's noise on top of the trend, not the trend itself.

## How often to actually check it

Monthly or quarterly is plenty — this isn't a number that benefits from daily attention, and checking it too often mostly just exposes you to short-term market noise in a way that can feel discouraging for no real reason. A spreadsheet with one row per month (assets, liabilities, net worth) takes a few minutes to update and turns an abstract feeling of "am I doing okay financially" into an actual trend line you can look at.

## What not to do with it

Don't compare your net worth to someone else's without knowing their starting point, their age, their debt situation, or their income — the number is only genuinely useful compared against *your own* past numbers, which is the entire reason tracking it over time matters more than any single snapshot. And don't let a temporarily negative number talk you out of also building an [emergency fund]({{ '/emergency-fund-how-much/' | relative_url }}) or investing — those are usually still worth doing in parallel with paying down debt, not after net worth turns positive, which could take years.

## The takeaway

Net worth isn't a status symbol, it's a diagnostic — assets minus debts, tracked over time, is the most honest single read on whether your overall finances are improving. Calculate it once to get a real starting point, however unglamorous the number, then check it again next month. The trend is the actual information; the first number is just where the trend starts.
